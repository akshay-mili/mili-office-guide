Everything that runs through your custodians

Mili reads your custodian data into everyday advice and automates the paperwork that follows — for one new account, or for every client of a firm you have just acquired.

Custodian work is usually described as forms. In practice it is three things: the data your advice is built on, the paperwork across an account's whole life, and the same paperwork run across an entire book when you buy a firm. Mili covers all three from one client record.

Nothing moves without you

Every workflow that touches the outside world is two steps: Mili fills the form, you see the PDF before it goes anywhere; Mili drafts the packet, you release it. Missing inputs are named rather than assumed, and where two of your own documents disagree, the conflict is flagged instead of resolved silently.

And custodian operations is one part of the platform. The same engine that fills a transfer form also runs prospect research, meeting preparation, book-wide client queries and full planning engagements — estate, tax, divorce, family, education — from the same client record.

Account opening, end to end

One workflow of several, and the one that carries the most manual load. Nothing is re-keyed, and nothing leaves your desk without you seeing it.

  1. 01

    Collect the information

    Client details are pulled from conversations, inbound email, meeting notes and uploaded documents, then written into your CRM — no intake form to send and chase.

    Live
  2. 02

    Fill the custodian's own forms

    Data is auto-typed into the custodian's actual PDFs — new account application, IRA, managed account agreement, transfer of assets, beneficiary designation.

    Live
  3. 03

    Check it for good order

    Every packet is checked against the custodian's requirements before it goes out, so a missing signature or unfilled field is caught here rather than a week later.

    Live
  4. 04

    Correct and refill in conversation

    You supply the missing detail in chat and every affected form is rebuilt. Nothing is retyped and no version drifts out of sync.

    Live
  5. 05

    Write it back to your systems

    The account and everything the documents revealed are written into your CRM and planning software — Salesforce, Advyzon, Redtail, eMoney.

    Live
  6. 06

    Prepare the packet for signature

    Signature and initial markers placed automatically, the packet assembled in signing order and sent for e-signature, with automated reminders until it is complete.

  7. 07

    Submit, then watch for rejections

    The packet submitted to the custodian and not-in-good-order alerts surfaced against the specific field that failed.

Repapering an acquired book

Acquisition is where custodian paperwork stops being a task and becomes a project.

One new client is a packet. An acquired firm is several hundred of them — all at once. Every household needs transfer paperwork, a new advisory agreement and fresh beneficiary designations inside the same window, and the deal economics depend on how fast the assets actually move. It is the same workflow as a single account opening, run across a book instead of a household.

  1. 01

    Ingest the acquired book

    Client records, account lists, agreements and statements read out of the selling firm's systems and document stores, however they are held.

    Live
  2. 02

    Match against your existing book

    Households reconciled and duplicates surfaced before a single form is generated, so shared clients are not papered twice.

    In build
  3. 03

    Build the packet per household

    Transfer forms, the advisory agreement and beneficiary designations filled from what came across in the acquisition — per household today, across the book next.

    LiveBulk in build
  4. 04

    Send for signature across the book

    Every household out in one pass, each chased automatically until its packet is complete, rather than an operations team working a spreadsheet.

    In discussion
  5. 05

    Track by household and by account

    Completion, outstanding signatures and not-in-good-order returns on one view, so you know what is actually holding the transition up.

    In discussion
  6. 06

    Write the acquired clients into your systems

    CRM, planning software and reporting populated from the transition itself, so the book arrives ready to service rather than needing a data project afterwards.

    Live

The building blocks — reading documents, filling custodian forms, good-order checking and writing back to your systems — are the same ones that run a single account opening. What is being built is running them across a book at once.
